Kit checklist for your first class

Exactly what to bring (and what to leave at home) for your free trial — written by the people who watch you walk in nervous.

Every Monday morning we get the same five questions from people booked in for their trial that week. Here are the answers, in order.

What clothes should I wear?

  • Comfortable workout clothes. Gym shorts or jogging bottoms, a t-shirt or vest.
  • No zips or buttons. They tear gis and dig into skin during clinch work.
  • Lighter is better. You'll sweat a lot. Cotton holds it; technical fabric doesn't.

Do I need to buy anything before my first class?

No. We lend you everything you need for the trial:

  • Gloves (Boxing / Muay Thai)
  • A clean borrowed gi (BJJ)
  • Hand wraps if you need them
  • Shin guards, if your class involves clinch sparring

Most members buy their own gloves and gi after the second week — it's worth it once you commit, but you don't need to spend a penny to find out if you like it.

What do I bring myself?

  • Water bottle — you'll need it
  • Small towel — for after class
  • Flip-flops — if you'd rather not walk on the changing-room floor barefoot
  • A change of clothes — most members shower before they leave

What about food and timing?

  • Eat at least 90 minutes before class. A small meal, nothing heavy.
  • Hydrate during the day — most people show up under-hydrated.
  • Aim to arrive 10 minutes early. A coach will meet you at reception.

What if I have an injury?

Tell the coach before class starts. We have alternative drills for everything from a tweaked back to a healing shoulder. We'd much rather modify your session than have you sit it out.

What if I'm really nervous?

You're not the only one. About a quarter of the people in your class will be on their first or second session. Tell the coach you're nervous when you arrive — they'll buddy you up with someone friendly and check in on you between drills.

Bring a friend if it helps. Their first class is also free.

---

One last thing: don't worry about being "out of shape". The biggest gains in martial arts come in the first six months, regardless of where you start. Showing up is the hardest part.
