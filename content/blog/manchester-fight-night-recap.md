Last Saturday we sent **fourteen members** to their first-ever interclub Muay Thai event in Manchester. Thirteen of them had been training for less than a year. None had ever stepped into a ring before.

By the end of the night, eleven walked away with a "win" — not the official kind, because interclubs aren't scored — but the kind that matters more: they'd done the thing that scared them.

Here's what we noticed.

## Confidence beats cardio

The fittest member on the card wasn't the one who looked best. The one who *trained the most rounds with people they didn't know* was. Sparring with strangers in your home gym is the closest thing to competition prep that exists, and we'll be ramping that up for the next event.

## The warm-up matters more than the gameplan

Every coach in the corner had a gameplan. Every gameplan went out the window after thirty seconds in the ring. The members who **warmed up properly** — twelve minutes of progressive intensity, not a quick stretch — looked sharp from the bell.

## Cornering is a skill

We rotated three coaches across the cards. By the eighth bout we'd settled on three rules:

1. **One voice in their ear.** Two coaches shouting different things during a 60-second break is useless.
2. **One adjustment per round.** Tell them the *one* thing to do differently. Not three.
3. **Praise first, fix second.** "Great jab in round one. Now keep it sharper in round two."

This is going into our coach training programme.
