Most martial-arts gyms are built for the people already in them. The schedule favours advanced students. The drills assume prior knowledge. The vibe — even when nobody intends it — leaves a new member feeling like they've walked into a private club.

We do the opposite at Forge.

## Rule 1 — Beginners get their own classes

Three nights a week, every discipline runs a **dedicated beginner programme**. No mixed levels. No live sparring. Just structured fundamentals taught at the pace of the slowest person in the room.

What does that look like? On a typical Monday at 6pm:

- 10 minutes of mobility — most people have never moved this way
- 25 minutes of one technique, broken into three steps
- 15 minutes of partner drilling, light resistance only
- 10 minutes of cool-down, questions, water

You leave knowing one thing well, and you feel safe. That's the whole point.

## Rule 2 — Your coach knows your name by class three

Every new member is paired with a coach for their first month. We track who you trained with, what you struggled with, and what you said you wanted to get out of training. After every class your coach checks in for sixty seconds — that's it. But it makes the difference between feeling like a customer and feeling like a member.

## Rule 3 — Progress is personal

We don't run formal grading until you've had at least 8 weeks of consistent attendance. There's no pressure to "compete" against the people next to you. The only benchmark is **last-month-you**.

Members who've trained elsewhere often tell us this is what hooked them. Nobody is showing off. Everybody is improving.

## What to expect on day one

- Arrive **10 minutes early**. A coach will meet you at reception.
- Wear loose, comfortable clothes — gym shorts and a t-shirt are fine.
- Bring a water bottle. We provide everything else for the trial — gloves, gi, the lot.
- Tell the coach if you're nervous. They've heard it before, and they have a routine for it.

Most people leave their first class with the same feeling: "that was easier than I thought, and harder than I expected."

Then they come back.
